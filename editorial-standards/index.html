Editorial Standards

How ScalpClock Content Is Created

ScalpClock's educational articles and guides are written by Tavares Vickers, the platform's founder. Here's how that content gets made, updated, and kept accurate.

Educational content, not personalized financial advice. Everything ScalpClock publishes — articles, guides, lessons, live signals, and Chart Replay commentary — is general educational material about how markets and trading strategies work. It is not personalized investment advice, and ScalpClock is not a registered investment adviser or broker-dealer. Trading involves risk, including the risk of loss. Always do your own research and consider your own financial situation before making trading decisions.

Who Writes This

Authorship

ScalpClock's blog posts and options-trading guides are written by Tavares Vickers, the site's founder and creator. Each article carries a visible byline near the top linking to his author profile, and each article's structured data identifies him as the author so it's clear — to readers and to search engines alike — who wrote it.

Keeping Content Current

How articles are updated

Each article displays the date it was last updated. When market mechanics, platform features, or terminology change, existing articles are revised in place rather than left to go stale — the "last updated" date reflects the most recent substantive edit, not a cosmetic touch-up.

If an error is found in a published article, it's corrected as soon as it's identified.

Sourcing

How facts and examples are chosen

Explanations of trading concepts (options mechanics, chart patterns, technical indicators, risk management principles) are based on standard, widely-documented market definitions rather than any single proprietary source. Chart examples used to illustrate a concept are either generated sample price action or drawn from ScalpClock's own tools (live signals, Chart Replay) and are labeled as illustrative examples, not as guaranteed outcomes or performance claims.

Outdated Information

When something changes

Trading platforms, tax treatment, and market structure details can change over time. If part of an article becomes outdated, it's updated rather than left as-is, and the article's "last updated" date is refreshed to reflect the correction. Readers relying on any time-sensitive detail (fees, contract specifications, regulations) should verify it against a current primary source, such as their broker or the relevant exchange.

Risk Disclosures

How risk is presented

Options trading carries real risk of loss, and strategies that limit risk in one way often introduce a different tradeoff elsewhere. ScalpClock's guides aim to explain those tradeoffs plainly rather than minimize them, and none of ScalpClock's educational material states or implies a specific profit outcome from following any strategy or using any ScalpClock tool.
